## 1. Does COPPA apply to us?

- COPPA covers an **operator** of a website or online service **directed to children under 13** (or one with actual knowledge it collects from under-13s). "Child" = under 13 (§ 312.2).
- Book Buddies targets ages 6–11 with picture-based quizzes, so it is **child-directed** under the multi-factor test (subject matter, visual content, animated characters, child-oriented activities, intended audience, marketing materials) (§ 312.2).
- **Consequence:** the "mixed audience" age-screen route is not available to us. There is no age gate that lets us skip COPPA for some users. The child experience must comply for every child user.
- **OPEN (`COPPA-operator-status`):** "Operator" is limited to commercial services and excludes nonprofits that are outside FTC Act § 5. Whether an independent project run by the client with the team counts is a legal question, because it depends on who the operator is and whether any nonprofit exclusion could apply. Dr. Yang's own follow-up already treats COPPA as the governing frame, so the working assumption is **we build as if covered**.
- **Timing:** the preamble sets the effective date 60 days after the final Rule is published and full compliance 365 days after publication (Part II.I.2.c). The file does not state the publication date. It refers to the "pre-2025 Rule", and its safe-harbor reporting deadline is April 22, 2028 "and every three years thereafter" (§ 312.11), which points to a 2025 publication and full compliance in 2026. Treat the revised rule as binding and design to it, not to the pre-2025 rule. *(Inferred; confirm the publication date.)*

## 2. What counts as personal information (PI)

Anything below, collected online from a child, is PI (§ 312.2). Items in **bold** are ones this product is close to.

| PI category                                                                                                                | Book Buddies relevance                                                                    |
|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| First and last name; home address; phone number                                                                            | Never collect from a child.                                                               |
| Online contact information (email, IM/VoIP/video handle; a mobile number only for texting a parent about consent)          | Never collect from a child. Parent email is collected from the parent, not the child.     |
| **Screen or user name, if it works like contact information**                                                              | Child aliases must not be usable to contact a person, inside or outside the app.          |
| Government ID numbers                                                                                                      | Never.                                                                                    |
| **Persistent identifier that recognizes a user over time (cookie ID, IP address, device ID, customer number)** | Our internal child ID is one (§ 312.2, "Personal information" (7)). So are an IP address or device ID if sign-in or session records keep one. See § 4. |
| Photo, video, or audio containing a child's image or voice                                                                 | Do not allow uploads or voice input.                                                      |
| Geolocation precise enough for street and town                                                                             | Do not collect.                                                                           |
| Biometrics (fingerprint, voiceprint, faceprint, gait, etc.)                                                                | Do not collect. New in this rule.                                                         |
| **Any other information about the child or their parents, collected from the child and combined with an identifier above** (§ 312.2, "Personal information" (11)) | Interests, favorite books, reactions, shelf and shelf history, ratings, the derived interest profile and usage records, once tied to the internal child ID. |

**Design consequence:** treat everything attached to a logged-in child profile as PI. The last row makes "anonymous child profile" a *privacy* feature but not a *COPPA exit*. A pseudonymous profile with a persistent ID and a reading history is still regulated. **ASSUMPTION (`COPPA-conservative-pi`):** we do not rely on any argument that account-linked quiz, shelf or interest data falls outside the definition. `BR-kid-data-stored` is the item-by-item inventory with the purpose of each. The admin's lookup of one child's usage data by internal ID shows PI even though it carries no name (`COPPA-admin-blind`). **ASSUMPTION (`COPPA-parent-note-covered`):** item (11) covers information collected *from the child*, and the parent note is typed by the parent, so it may fall outside the definition; we treat it as covered anyway (consent, notice, retention, deletion), which is the conservative choice.

### 3.1 Verifiable parental consent (VPC) before collection

|------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------|
| `COPPA-vpc-before-collection`      | Obtain VPC **before** any collection, use or disclosure of a child's PI, including consent to any later material change in practices.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               | § 312.5(a)(1)                                         |
| `COPPA-parent-first-account`       | The parent's account and consent flow must exist before a child profile can collect anything. A child must not enter an email, name or quiz answer before consent is recorded.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      | § 312.5(a), (c)                                       |
| `COPPA-no-persistent-id-exception` | Do not rely on the "persistent identifier only" exception for the child experience. It applies only when **no other PI** is collected, and the quiz, shelf and ratings are other PI.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                | § 312.5(c)(7)                                         |
| `COPPA-consent-method`             | Use a consent method "reasonably calculated … to ensure that the person providing consent is the child's parent." | `COPPA-email-plus-condition`       | "Email plus" and "text plus" are allowed **only if we do not "disclose" children's PI** (release to third parties, or make it public in identifiable form). | `COPPA-consent-record`             | Store a durable consent record: who, when, method, notice version shown, scope (including whether third-party disclosure was separately consented). Needed to prove VPC and to trigger re-consent on change.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        | § 312.2 "obtaining verifiable consent"; § 312.5(a)(1) |
| `COPPA-reconsent-on-change`        | Material changes to collection, use or disclosure (new data fields, new vendor, new feature that shares data) need fresh direct notice and consent before they go live.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             | §§ 312.4(b), 312.5(a)(1)                              |
| `COPPA-consent-pending-cleanup` | If we hold a parent's or child's name/contact only to obtain consent and consent does not arrive within a reasonable time, **delete it**. The consent notice must say so. "Reasonable time" must be a number: the client accepted 14 days as a development placeholder, configurable and not hardcoded, for legal review to set (client answers 2026-10-28, Q9; `BR-coppa-retention`). | §§ 312.5(c)(1), 312.4(c)(1)(vii) |
| `COPPA-no-school-consent`          | The rule text has no school-consent exception: the FTC proposed one, did **not** finalize it, and says it will keep enforcing ed-tech cases under its existing guidance, which is not in `coppa-full`. Design choice: the parent consents; teachers can flag but not consent. Whether school use would need more is a question for counsel.                                                                                                                                                                         | Preamble § I.A                                        |

### 3.2 Separate consent for non-integral third-party disclosure

| ID                                  | Requirement                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     | Rule                            |
|-------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------|
| `COPPA-separate-disclosure-consent` | Give the parent the option to allow collection and use **without** allowing disclosure to third parties, and get **separate** VPC for any disclosure that is not integral to the service. It may be asked up front or later, when the child first reaches a feature that needs it. The consent has to be freely given, informed, specific and an affirmative act distinct from the main consent. No manipulative choice design. | § 312.5(a)(2); preamble II.D.1  |
| `COPPA-never-integral`              | The FTC says these are **not** integral and always need separate consent: disclosure for money or other consideration, for advertising, and **to train or develop AI**.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         | Preamble II.D.1.b               |
| `COPPA-integral-listed`             | Disclosures that are integral (hosting, a necessary service provider) must still be **named in the notices**, and we must be able to tell integral from non-integral.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           | §§ 312.4(c)(1)(iv), 312.4(d)(2) |

**Direct hit on the product:** the client first wanted AI/ML recommendation from the start; for V1 the client accepted a rules-based recommender run entirely inside the app, with AI later (client answers 2026-10-28, Q7). Releasing child data to a vendor that uses it for its own purposes, or letting a vendor train a model on it, triggers separate parental consent and ends email-plus eligibility (preamble II.D.1.b says disclosure "to train or otherwise develop artificial intelligence technologies" is not integral). A vendor that only runs our recommendations for us is a service provider, not a "third party", if it uses the data for nothing else, and "personalize the content" is one of the listed internal-operations functions (§ 312.2, "Support for the internal operations of the website or online service" (1)(iii)); we would still need its written assurances (`COPPA-vendor-assurances`). Whether a given vendor qualifies is a legal call (`OI-20`). Keeping rules, scoring and any ML in our own environment avoids the question.

### 3.5 Data minimization and no conditioning

| ID                                | Requirement                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            | Rule                                    |
|-----------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------|
| `COPPA-minimize` | Collect from a child **no more than reasonably necessary** for each activity, and justify every stored field (`BR-kid-data-stored` gives the purpose of each). The rule text of § 312.7 only bars *conditioning participation* on disclosing more information than is reasonably necessary. The FTC reads it as an **outright prohibition on collecting more than is reasonably necessary, and says consent does not cure over-collection** (preamble, Part II.E, the discussion of § 312.7). Design to the FTC's reading. | § 312.7; preamble II.E |
| `COPPA-no-conditioning-on-extras` | Do not make a child's participation depend on giving more PI than the activity needs. If the parent declines optional disclosure, access stays.                                                                                                        | §§ 312.7, 312.5(a)(2)                   |
| `COPPA-no-free-text-pii` | The child UI has no free-text boxes. **Met:** the client removed child free text on 2026-10-28; a child answers only with structured choices (`BR-child-structured-responses`). The one free-text field is the parent's 250-character note, with a prompt not to include identifying information (`BR-parent-note`), screened by an internal word list (`BR-note-safety-flagging`). If a free-text field is ever added for a child, strip PI **in real time, with no storage**, before display or save. | § 312.2 "collects" (2); preamble II.I.1 |
| `COPPA-no-media-sensors`          | No photo/avatar-from-photo, voice input, camera, or location. If voice search is ever added, the narrow audio exception requires: only a child-request response, no other PI, no disclosure, deletion immediately, plus the online-notice description. | §§ 312.5(c)(9), 312.4(d)(4)             |

### 3.7 Security and vendors

| ID                        | Requirement                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     | Rule           |
|---------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|----------------|
| `COPPA-security-program`  | A **written information security program** with safeguards proportional to sensitivity, size and scope. ### 3.8 Engagement and notifications (not binding, but watched)

- The FTC **declined** to add a ban on using internal-operations data to "encourage or prompt use," and declined to restrict repeat-contact notifications. It also said it **may enforce under FTC Act § 5** against practices that prolong children's use in harmful ways (preamble II.B.4.c, II.D.5).
- `COPPA-no-child-nudges`: keep the existing product stance (no streaks, counts, leaderboards, comparisons). Do not add push notifications, emails, or re-engagement prompts to children. Notifications go to the **parent**: the weekly reminder asking whether a child finished a book is sent to the parent only (`FR-NOT-weekly-reminder`). The child dashboard (`UC-SHLF-kid-dashboard`) is a screen the child opens, not a prompt, and is written to show no counts, streaks or comparisons (`OI-31`).
- Using the parent's email for the weekly reminder is a use of contact information the parent gave for the account; it must be named in the notice (§ 312.4). The narrow exception in § 312.5(c)(2) is for services that collect no other child PI, so it does not apply to us.
- If any child-directed notification is ever proposed, the contact-info exceptions are narrow: respond to a *specific child request*, no other use, no disclosure, and parent notice (§ 312.5(c)(3)–(4); § 312.4(c)(3)).
- The persistent-identifier exception also bars using that data "to contact a specific individual … amass a profile … or any other purpose" outside the listed internal operations (§ 312.2).

## 4. The disclosure line (the one design decision that drives cost)

"Disclose" = (1) releasing child PI in identifiable form to anyone other than us or a provider supporting internal operations, or (2) **making it publicly available in identifiable form** (post, message board, chat room, email service) (§ 312.2).

- **Nothing moves one child's data to another person or to the public.** Groups, Buddy Picks and peer sharing were cut (`CO-no-social`, glossary Retired Terms). A child's shelf, interests and dashboard are visible only to that child and the linked parents. A flagged parent note is read only by an authorized admin, inside the operator.
- **Data that does leave the app goes to service providers only:** the host, the database provider and the email sender for the consent and confirmation emails. Each needs written assurances before it holds child PI (`COPPA-vendor-assurances`, § 312.8(c)), and none may use the data for anything else.
- **No report to outside parties in V1.** The client wants no automatic action and no report to authorities or any service (client answers 2026-10-28, Q4, Q6). A report would be a disclosure and would need counsel first (`OI-18`).
- Staying out of "disclose" keeps **email-plus** valid for consent (§ 3.1, § 312.5(b)(2)(viii)), which the client accepted for V1 (Q13), and avoids the separate-consent step (§ 3.2). If richer sharing, an outside AI vendor or an outside moderation service is later added, the consent method must move up to a stronger one (payment, ID check, KBA, video or phone) and separate disclosure consent comes into play. Redo this section first.
- `COPPA-picks-not-disclosure` (an alias-plus-book item shown to approved group members is not a disclosure) is **retired** on 2026-10-29 with the groups. The identifier is kept so old references still resolve.

## 7. What the rule does *not* require of us (so we don't over-build)

- No age gate (we are fully child-directed, not mixed audience).
- No published security program (only the retention policy and the notices are published).
- No separate children's-only security or retention document if general ones cover children's data.
- No platform-based consent mechanism (the FTC did not adopt one).
- Safe-harbor membership is optional. It is a possible compliance route later, not a requirement now.
